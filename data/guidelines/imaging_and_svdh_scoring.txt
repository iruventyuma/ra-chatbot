X-ray imaging of the hands and feet is a standard tool for assessing
structural joint damage caused by rheumatoid arthritis over time. Two of
the most important findings radiologists look for are bone erosion (BE)
and joint space narrowing (JSN).

Bone erosion refers to a break in the smooth cortical surface of the bone
near a joint, caused by inflamed synovial tissue eating away at the bone.
Joint space narrowing refers to a reduction in the visible gap between two
bones at a joint, which usually reflects thinning or loss of the
cartilage that normally cushions the joint.

The Sharp/van der Heijde (SvdH) scoring method is a widely used, detailed
system for quantifying both bone erosion and joint space narrowing at
specific, named joints in the hands, wrists, and feet, such as the
metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints, proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joints,
and various wrist bones. Each joint is scored individually and the scores
are summed to produce an overall damage score, which can be tracked over
time to see whether a patient's disease is progressing despite treatment,
or whether treatment is successfully slowing structural damage.

Because SvdH scoring is detailed and joint-specific, it is more sensitive
to small changes than a simple visual read of "erosion present or
absent," which makes it valuable both in clinical trials of new RA
therapies and in tracking individual patients with established disease.

When comparing a new X-ray to similar previously scored cases, it is
useful to look at cases with comparable erosion and narrowing patterns
across similar joints, since this can help contextualize how advanced or
early the structural findings in the new image appear relative to a
broader set of scored examples.
